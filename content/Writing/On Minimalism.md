Minimalism has helped me and changed my life in so many ways. 

On one sunny day in 2020, I stumbled upon an audiobook reading of Fumio Sasaki's _Goodbye, Things_. 
![[GoodbyeThings.jpg]]

Listening to this book officially coined the term in my brain. I began to get rid of things that I no longer needed and found a sense of peace in the action of decluttering, although sometimes too much (will get into that later).
# Timeline

## Childhood
Looking back, I believe that this minimalist mindset was due to my reliance on mental imagination, coming up multiple use cases for items that otherwise be deemed "single purpose". When I was around five years old, I had a predisposition towards being minimal with the toys I played with, a single plastic stick that held the barcode to a small rug and a vacuum box that my parents bought from Costco. Refusing to play with anything else, I relied on imagination to visualize that the stick as a sword and the box was either a turtle shell or a fortress to take cover under.

## Adolescence
Around 2010, I lived in a small room that did not have much storage space. A room with a twin size bed, a small folding table and chair to study. I used a repurposed bookshelf to store my books and folded clothes. Living in a small space really made me start noticing the amount of things in my room. The less things I had meant more space for living.

![[Pasted image 20260616225556.png]]
## University
When I was 18, I thought about commissioning to become an officer in the Marines. This began my obsession over military gear prep which involved making ranger rolls and tucking my sheets to emulate the bed style done in bootcamp. I spent hours reading and watching tutorials, this also led me to learn about first aid and overall survival preparedness.
 ![[bootcampbed.jpg]]

After deciding to attend community college and even during by bachelors', I tried to keep my items to a minimal in order to focus on my education. The less distractions there were meant the more capacity and time I had to pursue my interests.
# Extremes and Reaching Equilibrium
During my period of extreme decluttering, I began to develop an "ego" of sorts on how few things I owned. It came to a point where I was solely focused on getting rid of things rather than questioning if the item had any value to my quality of life. I felt a false sense of superiority knowing that I could survive on less compared to other people. In reality, no one cared if I had a hoarded home with millions of items or if I became a hermit with a single bowl, it was all inside my head.

Me boasting to the world after I got rid of 80% of my possessions:

![[Pasted image 20260618204841.png]]

I came to the realization that I was so focused on reducing that I forgot the original point of minimalism was to live an intentional life. To live in a way that would allow me to move and think freely, unencumbered from physical and mental clutter. Instead of treating minimalism like a game with a scorecard where the number of my possessions equaled the quality of life I was living, I began to deal with my possessions with a pragmatic approach. You need two jackets for the winter? Cool, keep it. You need three shoes to deal with different terrain? Awesome, enjoy the colors. As long as each item I possessed serves a purpose then all is good.

Most folks who live with the minimalist philosophy figure out that after the decluttering "high", you need to build a life that you would want to live. The point of minimalism was not to count how many items you have or don't have to feel a moral sense of superiority on others for their way of life, it was ultimately to remove all the distractions which would allow you to build a life that you would look forward to wake up.

I know everything is replaceable and that the things that surround me are tools, things that are meant to be used, art to be enjoyed, it allowed me to enjoy the items I possess. It's a similar feeling in games when you know you can use the items you collected since you will have more or the story is ending so why not have fun with what's in my inventory. If I do need to buy something then I make sure it's needed for the journey I chose. The last thing I want to do is to hinder myself because getting into a hobby or field requires acquiring more gear or items. If it serves a purpose then it's fair game.
# Advice for the Interested
My advice for folks who want to give minimalism a try is to envision your ideal future before you start decluttering. What do you want out of life and what items do you need in order to accomplish your goals?

Let's say you wanted to become a surfer, you would need a surfboard, wetsuits, and various tools to maintain your board and kit. If you wanted to become a hiker you would need hiking boots, a tent, bear canisters, perhaps a cooking kit. In order to determine what is required you have to envision what the 'end goal' would be. Once you do this, it will be really easy to identify what really matters in your life. This is one of the lessons I have learned from this endeavor, minimalism teaches you to cut the bulk of things that you once thought were nice to haves or even considered essential.
# Summary
Minimalism has been with me my entire life and I don't see it leaving anytime soon. Circumstances might change and I will gain and lose items in the ebb and flow of life but in the end, I know that things are things and you cannot take them with you once this game of life is over.

I have gotten by with less, so anything above baseline becomes a nice to have.
